Context file for Anthony's creative portfolio. Paste into projects so collaborators

## Who this is

improviser, facilitator and co-founder/treasurer of **Awkward Actors**, a short-form improv

Design taste runs to revelation and decoding — Obra Dinn, The Witness, Outer Wilds,

## The method

**A lot of this is toy-making** — in the sense Anthony set out in *Toy Software, Toy
Comedy* (June 2025): build a small version of something from scratch, start crude,
increment, and you come to understand it from the inside. Feynman's "what I cannot create,
I do not understand."

It explains a good part of the portfolio better than any grouping by subject:

- **Castles of Burgundy engine** — literally the chess-engine example from the essay.
- **Seams** — enumerate the pentomino space, dedup by symmetry; the toy reveals the shape of the whole set.
- **HaikuCraft** — take a poem apart to expose its structure.
- **Gibber** — build a language to understand language.
- **As I Recall** — built on Booker's seven basic plots, i.e. the underlying principles the essay says repetition surfaces.
- **Understudies** — public domain plays as scaffolding.

**But the mode has shifted since the essay was written, and the difference matters.**
Barreto's mechanism is the craft burden: you understand the chess engine because you bore
the cost of writing it. Anthony now works AI-assisted and directorial — imagining,
specifying, prototyping fast, often without inspecting the implementation closely. The
output is not crude; Seams is mature and the site's copy is better than most studios
manage.

So the knowledge is different in kind. Barreto's toys teach you **how things are made**;
these teach you **how things behave** — whether a mechanic is fun, whether a concept holds,
what shape a puzzle space is, whether anyone returns tomorrow. That's design knowledge, and
it's what a games business actually needs, but conflating it with implementation knowledge
blurs a real distinction.

The Feynman test relocates rather than disappearing: at the directorial level it becomes
**what I cannot specify, I do not understand.** Vague briefs produce mush, and the mush is
the signal.

**Where the frame stops.** It fits the play lane well and is silent on the shipping lane.
It fits Suggestion Machine badly — that's a tool solving a real operational problem on show
nights, not a system built to understand improv. It barely fits Classic Comic Art or Holy
Unexpected at all; those are craft and curation. Call it a frame covering perhaps
two-thirds of the map, useful for that part and not to be forced onto the rest.

**The more structural observation:** the directorial mode explains the *shape* of the
portfolio. Cheap, fast idea-to-prototype produces many starts — which is why there are
fifteen projects rather than two, and why the bottleneck sits at completion rather than
initiation.

**Still unresolved:** none of this explains why Anthony would *finish* anything. Barreto's
argument is that toys are joyful precisely because they carry no obligation. The frame
validates generation and offers nothing for month four of daily puzzle content.

## The drive

Reconnecting with his own creativity, and with a long-standing entrepreneurial itch —
wanting to work for himself and build a business, not just tinker. Enormous joy in
generating ideas, alongside a newer desire to focus and find what could actually become a
business.

The Idox takeover has completed and the share options have vested — that blocker is gone.
What remains is a judgement call about risk and readiness. Idox is currently genuinely
interesting because of AI acceleration, offering another domain to grow in. Worth noting:
an interesting job is harder to leave than a bad one, so it may be worth defining in
advance what evidence constitutes the button (a revenue figure, a subscriber count) rather
than waiting for a feeling to arrive unprompted.

## The lanes

Not identities — a way of allocating a genuinely scarce resource (evenings).

**Shipping lane** — exactly one thing at a time. The thing carried past the fun part: bugs,
churn numbers, month four of content when novelty is gone.

**Workshop lane** — real conditions, real users, no launch. Building under consequence
without the burden of strangers, pricing or onboarding.

**Play lane** — everything else. Consequence-free by design. No roadmaps, no domains
bought, no "this could be big." Suppressing this would cost the thing that makes the work
distinctive — and, per the method above, would cost the way Anthony learns.

**Constraint lane** — day job and freelance income. Not play, not shipping; it's what buys
time for both. Easy to leave off the map, which hides where the evenings go.

These are **internal vocabulary only**. They should never appear on the public site — a
visitor doesn't care which lane something is in, only what it is.

### The promotion rule

First test: **does this idea become a daily puzzle in the hub?** If yes, it's not a new
project — it's fuel. The Playhouse is an idea-consuming machine, and generating new puzzle
games is the business working as designed.

If no, it's a separate product, and the hard rule applies: it moves into the shipping lane
only when the shipping lane is **empty** — current thing launched and running without daily
attention. The moment business thinking starts happening about a play-lane project, it has
quietly jumped the queue.

## Patterns worth knowing

Two creative seams beneath the one method:

1. **Language at play** — haiku, phrases, emoji charades, invented language, cut-up poetry, improv suggestions, storytelling.
2. **Mathematical investigation** — pentominoes, problem-solving, investigative structures. Traceable to the PGCE maths training; those notes are an unmined seam. The brief when prospecting them is specific: **hunt for mechanics whose puzzle space can be enumerated and dedup-checked by machine.**

Cutting across both: **content cost.** Language games need hand-authoring; maths games can
generate their own content indefinitely. A hub anchored by two or three self-generating
puzzles has a far healthier maintenance profile and frees the hand-authored games to be
written when the mood is there. Currently only Seams and Always Never are on the
self-generating side — the shipping lane is stacked with games that need daily feeding.
**Getting to a second self-generating anchor is the highest-value structural work.**

The **systems games** (Castles, Brick-Brack-Bro, chess, 25 Squares) surface when the mood
is to *build* rather than to *launch*. Useful signal about which mode Anthony is in.

The **iPad / native strand** is a distribution question, not a project. Can't be answered
before there's return-rate data from the web versions.

The **full interactive game** is the long-horizon ambition — something a business funds,
not something that starts one.

## Two audiences, permanently distinct

**Makers and creative-rehabbers** read the log, the newsletter and the podcast. They're
here for process and honesty.

**Puzzle players** play the games. In the UK they skew heavily female and over 35. They do
not read devlogs.

Conflating these is the classic error. Building in public will not deliver players, and
judging it on player numbers makes it look like failure while it's succeeding at something
else. Players come from Reddit, press and search.

## Publishing and audience

**The site already says the right things.** anthonycreates.com states the drivers as
helping people reconnect with lost creativity, exploring adult playfulness, and creating
outlets for creative expression. That is Creative Rehab, already public. The copy is the
site's strongest asset — specific, confident, unlike anyone else's.

**The present-tense gap is being closed.** Live is implemented locally as a dated stream
for work in progress without redesigning the established portfolio. Launch now depends on
content, discoverability, deployment and final QA rather than further structural design.

**The naming problem.** Four names for one idea: Anthony Creates (site), Creative Rehab
(podcast), The Improvised Life (Substack), Classic Comic Art (Etsy). Someone who finds one
has no route to the others. Consolidating the writing under **Creative Rehab** is now
essentially free, since the Substack has no accumulated equity.

**Why the last newsletter attempt died: format, not platform.** All four posts were
essays — thesis-driven pieces, each effectively a small project. Expensive to write, so
when life got busy it stopped. The fix is making the send cheap: the log produces the
material, the newsletter assembles it, and the only fresh writing per issue is a
hundred-word note. Editorial line: *what building it taught me*, not a progress report.

**Creative Rehab, resolved and configured.** The Buttondown newsletter with replies enabled
*is* the community of practice in its lightest possible form — no platform to run, no
moderation burden. Signup, double opt-in, welcome, tagging, sending, reply tracking and
forwarding have all been tested successfully. The method section gives it a practice rather
than a vibe: **make small things, start crude, and notice what you learn from having made
them.** If it ever wants to become something more structured, the replies will say so.

### Launch playbook (from research into Wordle-adjacent games)

- **The share-grid is the growth engine**, not a feature. Spoiler-free, fits a social post, built in from day one.
- **Seed on Reddit** — r/wordle plus the interest-specific sub. Expect ~800/day initially; that's normal.
- **Unpaid press is the plateau-shifter.** Waffle's DAU stepped up with each write-up.
- **Paid ads don't work here.** Multiple creators on record; Nerdle reached a million players on $250.
- **Browser-first compounds** via SEO in a way app-store-first does not.
- **Cross-promotion with an established daily game is the highest-ROI single move** — Squaredle went 3k → 18k daily overnight from a Waffle collab.
- **Hubs work sequentially, never simultaneously.** Pick a hero game, get it to a loyal base, then bundle.

## Failure modes

- **Month three.** When the shipping lane gets tedious, the play lane will look extremely productive. It will feel like inspiration. It's avoidance.
- **Two shipping lanes on identity grounds.** Splitting because "I'm a technologist *and* an improviser" gives two half-speed lanes and a good story about why neither launched.
- **Essays.** The newsletter died of them once already. Hard word limits on log entries.
- **Redesigning instead of logging.** A site rebuild is a play-lane project wearing a shipping-lane costume.
- **A fifth name.** Consolidate, don't add.
- **Documents about the work.** Including this one. If it's being revised weekly, it's become the project.
